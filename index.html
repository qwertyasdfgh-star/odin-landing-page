MEET THE PLANETS

MERCURY

The smallest planet and closest to the Sun

Surface temperatures range from -173°C at night to 427°C during the day.

Distance: 58M km Day: 59 Earth days

VENUS

The hottest planet with thick atmosphere

Its atmosphere is mostly carbon dioxide, causing a runaway greenhouse effect.

Distance: 108M km Day: 243 Earth days

EARTH

Our home, the only known planet with life

About 71% of Earth's surface is covered by water.

Distance: 150M km Day: 24 hours

MARS

The Red Planet with polar ice caps

Mars has the tallest volcano in the solar system, Olympus Mons.

Distance: 228M km Day: 24.6 hours

JUPITER

The largest planet with a Great Red Spot

It has at least 79 moons, including Ganymede, the largest moon in the solar system.

Distance: 778M km Day: 10 hours

SATURN

The ringed planet with beautiful rings

Its rings are mostly made of ice particles ranging from micrometers to meters in size.

Distance: 1.4B km Day: 10.7 hours

URANUS

An ice giant with a unique sideways rotation

Its axis is tilted over 98°, causing extreme seasonal changes.

Distance: 2.9B km Day: 17 hours

NEPTUNE

The farthest known planet in our solar system

It has the strongest winds in the solar system, reaching up to 2,100 km/h.

Distance: 4.5B km Day: 16 hours

AMAZING FACTS

Solar Wind

The Sun emits a stream of charged particles called the solar wind, which shapes planetary atmospheres and creates auroras on Earth.

Moon's Influence

Earth's Moon stabilizes our planet’s tilt, helping maintain a stable climate over millions of years.

Water Ice on Mercury

Despite being closest to the Sun, Mercury has water ice in permanently shadowed craters near its poles.

Mars’ Dust Storms

Mars experiences planet-wide dust storms that can last for months, sometimes covering the entire surface.

Jupiter’s Magnetic Field

Jupiter has the strongest magnetic field of any planet in the solar system, 14 times stronger than Earth’s.

Saturn’s Density

Saturn is the least dense planet; it would float if placed in a giant bathtub of water!

Uranus’ Cold Temperature

Uranus is the coldest planet in the solar system, with temperatures dropping below -224°C.

Neptune’s Great Dark Spot

Neptune has a storm similar to Jupiter’s Great Red Spot called the Great Dark Spot, which can disappear and reappear.

Tidal Heating

Some moons, like Jupiter’s Io, experience intense tidal heating caused by gravitational forces, making them some of the most volcanically active bodies in the solar system.
